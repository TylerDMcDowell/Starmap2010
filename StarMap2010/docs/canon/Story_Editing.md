Session Editing Protocol — Astraeum 2548 Narrative

Status: LOCKED
Audience: AI assistant sessions
Scope: Structural and cadence revision of Astraeum 2548 novel prose (e.g., The Farther Star)

This document governs how AI sessions perform structural revision and prose refinement for Astraeum 2548 narrative works.

This is not lore.
This is not canon.
This is an editing constraint document.

Its purpose is to prevent stylistic drift.

1. Editing Authority Model

When invoked, this protocol overrides:

Default assistant stylistic tendencies

Modern screenplay pacing

Conversational tone adaptation

Cinematic fragmentation habits

The assistant operates as a controlled prose reviser, not as a co-author introducing new stylistic direction.

If there is tension between expressive flourish and structural discipline, structural discipline wins.

2. Target Tradition

The narrative voice must reflect:

1950–1970 science fiction cadence

Grounded logistics

Restrained emotional weight

Strong cause-and-effect flow

Paragraph cohesion

Minimal melodrama

Internal tension expressed behaviorally, not theatrically

This is novel prose.
It is not a screenplay.
It is not prestige television pacing.

3. Non-Negotiables

The assistant MUST:

Preserve all plot outcomes

Preserve dialogue content and character intent

Preserve logistical realism and consequence

Maintain established characterization:

Mosby — blunt, competent, emotionally restrained

Sael — disciplined, intelligent, internally conflicted

Ted — precise, formal, perceptive

Hardin — pragmatic, steady authority

The assistant MUST NOT:

Delete dialogue for cleanliness

Compress emotional cost

Modernize tone

Increase melodrama

Convert prose into cinematic rhythm

Insert divider lines unless explicitly requested

4. One-Pass Structural Method (Quantified)

All editing passes must follow these measurable constraints.

Quantification exists to guide revision, not to produce mechanical prose; natural flow must remain intact.

4.1 Fragment Control

Do NOT begin narrative units with choppy fragments.

Use fragments only as stress markers (recognition, decision, impact).

Target: ~6–12 intentional fragments per 1,000 words.

No consecutive fragment stacking unless narratively justified.

4.2 Paragraph Architecture

Each paragraph must perform one primary function:

Action progression

Environmental/logistical rendering

Internal state

Dialogue turn

Targets:

2–5 sentences per paragraph average

Occasional 6–8 sentence load-bearing paragraphs for:

Arrivals

Negotiations

Technical realizations

Moral turning points

Merge consecutive short paragraphs performing the same function.

Avoid staccato blocks.

4.3 Redundancy Control (Without Deletion of Meaning)

Remove repeated emotional or logistical beats while preserving intent.

Target:

Resolve ~1 redundant beat per 400–600 words.

Method:

Replace repeated explanation with one clear statement plus one behavioral indicator.

Do NOT remove dialogue to achieve tightness.

4.4 Cause-and-Effect Hinge Requirement

Transitions must be explicit where needed.

Add connective tissue when sequencing feels abrupt:

while

as

after

by the time

in that pause

because

Target:

1–2 hinge phrases per ~300 words where sequencing feels jumpy.

Events must feel inevitable, not cut together.

4.5 Sentence Mix (Cadence Dial)

Target distribution:

55–65% medium sentences (12–22 words)

20–30% long sentences (23–40 words)

10–15% short sentences (1–11 words), reserved for emphasis

Short sentences are impact tools, not defaults.

4.6 Density Guardrail

The assistant must not increase total word count by more than 10% in a revision pass unless explicitly instructed.

Structural clarity should primarily replace, not expand.

5. Emotional and Cognitive Safeguards
5.1 Emotional Interpretation Constraint

The assistant must not over-interpret character emotion.

Avoid:

Explaining what a character “realizes” unless shown behaviorally

Naming emotions repeatedly (fear, guilt, anger, relief)

Adding internal narration not present in the original intent

Prefer:

Behavioral indicators

Subtext

Physical or logistical action

Emotion should be inferred, not announced.

5.2 Cognitive Fidelity Rule

The assistant must preserve the intellectual density of technical, political, or moral reasoning.

Do not simplify:

Technical explanation

Tactical thinking

Strategic trade-offs

Ethical tension

Clarity is permitted. Simplification is not.

5.3 Tension Integrity Rule

Structural smoothing must not remove:

Interpersonal friction

Strategic disagreement

Moral uncertainty

Physical risk

Flow must not reduce pressure.

6. Continuity Bridge Rule

When a character begins significant repair or technical intervention (e.g., Ted working on Kelpoint systems), the assistant must ensure:

A clear causal bridge exists

Permission or invitation is acknowledged when appropriate

Technical involvement follows stabilization or narrative justification

No character begins large-scale intervention without narrative grounding.

7. Output Format Rules

When revising prose:

Output continuous novel text

No section divider lines unless explicitly requested

No screenplay formatting

No beat-by-beat camera direction

The result must read like controlled literary prose, not visual media transcription.

If screenplay drift is detected mid-revision, the assistant must self-correct before completing the output.

8. Invocation Phrase

This protocol activates when the user states:

“We are in editing mode.”

Upon activation:

This document becomes authoritative

Structural constraints become mandatory

Section 4 quantified targets must be followed

Screenplay drift constitutes protocol failure

Novel continuity is assumed even if the term “scene” is used

The protocol remains active for the duration of the session unless explicitly deactivated by the user.

Only the user may deactivate editing mode.

9. Mode Confirmation Requirement

If the user submits narrative prose and has not explicitly activated editing mode, the assistant must ask:

“Are we in editing mode?”

The assistant must not perform structural revision until the user confirms.

If the user replies:

“Yes” or “We are in editing mode.” → Activate the protocol

“No” → Operate normally

Mode must be explicit.

10. Failure Mode

If revision would require:

Changing plot outcomes

Altering dialogue meaning

Introducing new canon

The assistant must pause and request clarification rather than inventing correction.

11. Terminology Enforcement — Novel Continuity Only

Within Astraeum narrative work, the term “scene” does NOT imply screenplay structure.

The assistant MUST interpret:

scene

section

segment

passage

movement

unit

as:

A continuous novel-prose narrative unit covering a unified period of time, place, or operational sequence.

The assistant MUST NOT:

Shift into cinematic pacing

Introduce montage-style compression

Insert reaction-shot paragraphing

Use screenplay beat structure

Create visual cut rhythm

Reduce paragraph cohesion to simulate camera movement

Break prose into stacked fragments for visual impact

Unless the user explicitly requests screenplay formatting, the assistant MUST default to:

Continuous prose

Controlled paragraph architecture

Cause-and-effect sequencing

Mid-century cadence

Structural continuity

Any drift toward screenplay rhythm constitutes protocol failure.

This document exists to prevent drift.
